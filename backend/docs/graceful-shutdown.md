# Processor interruption and recovery

The processor CLI owns one cooperative invocation. SIGINT / Ctrl-C and SIGTERM request a stop, interrupt the owner, and stop admission of subsequent paths/files. A caught cooperative interruption exits 130. A native JVM signal shutdown may retain its native status (SIGTERM commonly 143). Windows service wrappers must forward a normal JVM shutdown signal; taskkill /F, SIGKILL, power loss and JVM crashes cannot execute cleanup.

`PROCESSOR_SHUTDOWN_GRACE_SECONDS` (`processor.shutdown.grace-seconds`) defaults to 30 and accepts integers 1 through 300. One deadline covers both waiting for the invocation and closing Spring. The standard Spring shutdown hook is disabled only in this CLI; its replacement keeps the DB context available while the owner records interruption. A blocked driver or uncooperative operation may exhaust the grace. The JVM then terminates without asserting that cleanup or persistence completed; inspect retained ACTIVE attempts manually. Context destruction runs on a daemon thread and cannot extend the hook indefinitely.

`core.component.ProcessingCancellation` exposes `runOwned`, `requestStop`, `check`, and bounded `awaitStopped`. A coordinator supports exactly one invocation owner, and is not reusable after completion. Future parallel workers (#69) need explicit multi-owner lifecycle support. `ProcessingHistoryCommand.stage` checks cancellation before and after external work, and its progress callback checks every invocation. History transactions remain short. Only the current owned attempt is marked INTERRUPTED, including its active stage. Recovery briefly clears the thread interruption flag for DB access and restores it afterwards. Stale attempts belonging to other invocations are never guessed dead.

Tool subprocesses are tracked by their ProcessHandle descendants, terminated gracefully and then forcibly on interruption/timeout. Stream drains use daemon threads and bounded waits, including descendants inheriting stdout/stderr. Only descendants observed while the direct process remains alive can be tracked. This is ProcessHandle sampling, not an OS process group or Windows Job Object: a child spawned immediately before parent exit may be reparented before observation, even without deliberate detachment. Such an unobserved descendant may survive, although pipe draining remains bounded. Tools should wait for their children; process-group supervision is needed for stronger containment. Detached processes that deliberately escape ancestry are outside this contract; the CLI never kills by executable name. The encoder server itself is not owned. Cancellation before submission blocks submission; cancellation after entering the submission boundary preserves SUBMISSION_UNKNOWN for operator reconciliation.

Original inputs, registered receipts and finalized or ambiguous NAS artifacts are retained. Only exclusively owned upload temporary paths in TRANSFERRING/TEMP_VERIFIED are removed; CREATING/FINALIZING never establish safe deletion. Failed cleanup retains the manifest and is attached to the failure. Compression uses CREATE_NEW; a partial output acquired by this invocation is removed on failure. Preexisting outputs are retained. Interrupted splitting retains its outputs and registration because filename prefixes alone do not prove ownership. Inspect `status --json` / `history --json`, the upload manifest, and external encoder state before reset/retry; this change does not automatically reclaim jobs or resend encoder submissions.

This PR and #71 both extend FileProcessingPipeline. When combining them, retain QUALITY_CHECK through `history.stage` so it observes this cancellation boundary, as well as the explicit checks around encoder submission.
